# What surface-comparison misses

## Metathesis, dual-face L3, and polysemy as cognate evidence in Afro-Asiatic

**Author:** Yassine Temessek · Temessek for Research, Publishing & Training
**Date:** 2026-05-24
**Status:** Draft v1 · open to peer review
**Bilingual:** [Arabic version](structural-revelations-article-ar.md)

---

## Abstract

Comparative-linguistic methodology, in both the Indo-European and Semitic traditions, has historically relied on **surface-skeleton matching** as the first filter for cognate identification: two words are candidate cognates if their consonant skeletons align (modulo a small set of known sound-shifts), and a cognate is rejected when the surface alignment fails. This paper argues that **surface-skeleton matching systematically misses three classes of genuine cognate**, each of which is recoverable through an operative-grammar reading anchored in phonosemantic seed-nuclei (the *Juthoor* framework: binary letter-charges that compose to letter-seeded meanings).

The three classes are: **(1) metathesis cognates** — pairs whose surface letter-order swaps obscure a shared underlying seed; **(2) dual-face L3 swaps** — pairs that share the same binary nucleus but differ only at the third (modifying) letter, where the framework's dual-face rule (each letter carrying two adjacent semantic faces) resolves them to the same compositional meaning; **(3) polysemy as cognate evidence** — single Egyptian words carrying multiple Arabic-distinct meanings on one shared nucleus, where the polysemy is itself the proof that one seed underlies the apparent semantic diversity.

We document **25 worked examples** across the three classes, drawn from a 200-entry Arabic ↔ Ancient-Egyptian roster, and argue that each is a structurally recoverable cognate that traditional methodology would mis-classify as either *unrelated* (for metathesis), *separately-derived* (for dual-face), or *coincidentally-similar* (for polysemy). The implication for Afro-Asiatic comparative work is that the operative-grammar layer is not optional — it is the only level at which these three classes surface.

## 1. The problem with surface-skeleton matching

When comparative linguists ask whether Arabic *lisān* (لِسان, "tongue") and Egyptian *ns* (likewise "tongue") are cognates, the standard pipeline is:

1. Extract the consonant skeleton of each form (Arabic: l-s-n; Egyptian: n-s).
2. Check whether the two skeletons align letter-for-letter under known sound-correspondence laws (here: l ↔ n? no documented Afro-Asiatic shift; the orders are wholly different).
3. If alignment fails, reject the cognate hypothesis.

By this procedure, *lisān* / *ns* are **not** cognates. The skeletons don't align. The order doesn't match. The traditional comparative method delivers a clear negative verdict.

Yet **Coptic ⲗⲁⲥ** (*las*, "tongue") preserves the l consonant that hieroglyphic Egyptian dropped in writing. The l is *there* in the spoken language — it was just absent from the conservative consonantal hieroglyphic script. The comparison should be:

- Arabic: l-s-n
- Coptic: l-a-s
- Hieroglyphic Egyptian: n-s (representing the same underlying root with metathesis between the n and final consonants)

Once we look beyond the surface to the **underlying binary nucleus**, both forms are saying the same thing: the **s-n** "fine-streaming flow + inner resonance" seed (the tongue's gesture in speech), which can present in either order. The framework's verdict: **same root, same seed, recovered via metathesis**.

This is one example of a broader pattern. Surface-skeleton matching misses three classes of cognate. We document each below.

## 2. Class 1 · Metathesis cognates

**Definition.** Pairs whose surface forms differ only in the order of the consonants composing the root, where the underlying binary nucleus and the compositional reading are identical.

**Why surface-matching misses these.** Traditional methodology requires positional alignment of consonants. A C₁-C₂-C₃ sequence in language A cannot be cognate with a C₂-C₁-C₃ or C₂-C₃-C₁ sequence in language B under standard rules, because the position-by-position match fails.

**Why the framework recovers them.** The binary nucleus is defined by the combination of letter-charges, not by their order. When two languages preserve the same charge-combination but differ in surface ordering, the framework still reads the same seed-meaning. The structural-revelation evidence is: a third descendant (often Coptic for Egyptian, or a divergent Arabic dialect) preserves the original order, confirming that the metathesis is real and post-dates the proto-form.

### Worked example 1.1 · لِسان (lisān) ↔ Egyptian *ns* ↔ Coptic ⲗⲁⲥ

- **Forms.** Arabic l-s-n; Egyptian n-s (consonants only in hieroglyphic); Coptic l-a-s.
- **Binary nucleus:** س-ن / s-n — *fine streaming flow + inner resonance emitted from inside* — exactly the tongue's action in speech.
- **+ ل (the bridge that extends):** the bridging-attachment that streams resonance.
- **Cognate verdict.** STRONG · metathesis cognate. The Coptic restoration of the l is the smoking-gun evidence: the l was always there in the root; the hieroglyphic writing system simply did not record the weak liquid in its consonantal skeleton.

### Worked example 1.2 · أَنف (ʾanf) ↔ Egyptian *fnḏ*

- **Forms.** Arabic ʾ-n-f ("nose"); Egyptian f-n-ḏ ("nose").
- **Binary nucleus:** نف / fn (metathesis on L1-L2) — *inner resonance + parting-through-the-breath* — the organ where the breath parts inward.
- **Cognate verdict.** STRONG · metathesis cognate. Both languages preserve the n + labial-fricative pair that names the nasal/breath organ; only the position of the labial differs.

### Worked example 1.3 · Egyptian *snt* polysemy (cross-link to §4 below)

Egyptian *snt* simultaneously means "tooth, sister, second-of-a-pair." Arabic preserves the three meanings on distinct surface roots: سِنّ (s-n-n, tooth), أُخت (ʾ-ḫ-t, sister), ثاني (th-n-y, second). Two of the three Arabic roots (سِنّ and ثاني) preserve the s/th + n nucleus directly; the third (أُخت) is a diverged kinship stem of the same nucleus. Egyptian's polysemy reveals that the underlying seed is *one* — *matched-pair-by-shared-fine-source* — and Arabic's three surface roots are different morphological developments of the same nucleus. This is a metathesis-adjacent case where surface divergence hides nucleus-identity.

### What the methodological literature missed

Standard Semitic-Egyptian comparative work (Cohen, Orel-Stolbova, Ehret) has noted *some* metathesis cases but lacks a principled mechanism for *predicting* when metathesis is the resolution. The framework's mechanism — the binary nucleus is unordered until L3 attaches and selects a face — makes metathesis a *predicted* phenomenon, not an after-the-fact accommodation. When two surface forms share the same letter-charges in different orders, and both yield the same operative reading, the prior probability of *random* metathesis-and-reconvergence is vanishingly small. Cognate-by-metathesis is the parsimonious conclusion.

## 3. Class 2 · Dual-face L3 swaps

**Definition.** Pairs that share an identical binary nucleus (L1 + L2) but differ at the third letter (L3), where both L3 candidates belong to the same articulator class and pick adjacent semantic faces of that articulator. Both forms read to the same compositional meaning under the framework's dual-face rule.

**Why surface-matching misses these.** A two-of-three consonant match (with the third differing) is conventionally treated as a *partial* cognate at best, or as evidence of *separate derivation*. Without a principled account of how the third letter modifies the binary seed, there is no way to argue that two different L3 candidates yielding the same final meaning should be considered the same word.

**Why the framework recovers them.** The dual-face rule (developed for Arabic-internal trilateral root analysis) states that every letter carries two adjacent semantic faces of the same physical articulation, and the L3 of a trilateral root selects which face is active. When two Afro-Asiatic descendants pick *different* L3 candidates from the same articulator-class family, both can compose with the shared L1-L2 nucleus to yield the same final reading. The framework recognises these as *one root in two faces*, not two separate words.

### Worked example 2.1 · رود ↔ روض

- **Forms.** Arabic رود (r-w-d, "trodden path, gentle walking") and روض (r-w-ḍ, "open meadow, cleared garden"). Egyptian preserves *rwd* (the ground-level cleared path) and *rwḏ* (the open-ground variant).
- **Shared nucleus:** رو / r-w — *running-flow + binding* — the pathway-of-passage.
- **L3 dual-face.** د (settled fixity, dental-emphatic family) ↔ ض (drawing-together with weight, spreading face of the same dental-emphatic family). Both are *dental-emphatic* articulator-class consonants, picking adjacent semantic faces — "settled-down anchor" and "weighted-spread expanse" — which both compose with the *running-binding* seed to give *a passable, navigable open way*.
- **Cognate verdict.** PERFECT · dual-face cognate. The dental-emphatic class shift (د/ض) is one of the most reliable Afro-Asiatic intra-family alternations.

### Worked example 2.2 · واسخ ↔ واسع

- **Forms.** Egyptian *wsḫ* "wide, spread" and Arabic واسع (w-s-ʿ) "wide, expansive."
- **Shared nucleus:** سخ / sḫ (Egyptian) ↔ سع / sʿ (Arabic) — but both build on the same و-prefixed *bound extension* base.
- **L3 dual-face.** خ (rarefying piercing through, throat fricative) ↔ ع (depth-grip intensity, throat). Both are *throat-family* consonants, picking adjacent faces — "breaking-through outward" (خ) and "emerging-from-depth" (ع) — which both compose with the *bound-extension* seed to give *the bound expanse breaking out into width*.
- **Cognate verdict.** DUAL-FACE PAIR. A textbook within-family L3 alternation, structurally identical to the Arabic-internal dual-face cases (where the same root takes two semantic faces depending on context).

### Worked example 2.3 · جَهل (ʾignorance) ↔ خم (cheat) on shared خم nucleus

- **Forms.** Arabic جَهل (j-h-l, "ignorance") under the framework's reading actually rests on a خم-related seed via the *piercing-of-sealed-mass* semantic axis. Egyptian *ḫm* "to not know" sits on exactly the same binary nucleus as *ḫm* "to break open the sealed thing."
- **Shared nucleus:** خم / ḫm — *piercing into the gathered-and-sealed mass* — what was held shut is now broken into.
- **Two semantic descendants on one seed.**
  - **Ignorance:** the knowledge-mass is *sealed* (you cannot pierce into it; you do not know).
  - **Cheating:** the trust-mass is *broken* (you have pierced into what someone held closed).
- **Cognate verdict.** Both Arabic and Egyptian *use the same خم nucleus* for both senses, despite the surface words appearing unrelated. The framework reads this as one seed expressing on two semantic faces of the same *piercing-sealed* gesture.

### What the methodological literature missed

Cohen's *Dictionnaire des racines sémitiques* and other classical comparative-Semitic compendia treat root-variants like د/ض or т/ţ as separate roots, recording them on different pages even when the semantic field is identical. The dual-face rule re-unifies these as one underlying root with L3-selected faces. This is not a re-interpretation of phonology — it is a recognition that the comparative-Semitic literature already implicitly contains these cases as a "doublet" pattern (روض / رود, شُكر / شَكر, طاعة / دعوة, etc.), and that the doublet pattern is *predicted* by the dual-face rule rather than being an unexplained quirk.

## 4. Class 3 · Polysemy as cognate evidence

**Definition.** When a single word in language A carries multiple meanings that correspond to distinct words in language B, and all of those meanings can be derived from a *single* shared binary nucleus, the polysemy itself is evidence that the underlying seed is one (and that language B's surface diversity is a later development).

**Why surface-matching misses these.** Polysemy in one language is treated as a problem to be disambiguated in dictionary-making, not as data about cognate-identification. Standard comparative methodology compares form-to-form, not form-to-meaning-cluster.

**Why the framework recovers them.** The framework reads meaning *from* the nucleus, so when a single nucleus reads to multiple final meanings (each delivered cleanly under composition with different L3s or in different morphological contexts), it predicts polysemy as a natural consequence. When that prediction is confirmed by the data, the polysemy becomes positive evidence that the framework has correctly identified the seed.

### Worked example 3.1 · Egyptian *snt* = tooth + sister + second

- **Egyptian word.** *snt*
- **Three Arabic-distinct meanings:** سِنّ (tooth), أُخت (sister, feminine of "brother" sn → snt), ثاني (second-of-a-pair).
- **Shared binary nucleus:** سن / sn — *fine streaming flow + inner resonance emitted* — the *matched-pair-by-shared-fine-source* seed.
- **How the three meanings derive from one seed:**
  - **Tooth** — the fine bone that pierces (the "sharp matched-pair" axis: teeth come in matched pairs that share the same sharpening).
  - **Sister** — the kinship-by-shared-source counterpart (the "matched-twinned-pair-by-shared-line" axis).
  - **Second** — the matched-twin-of-the-first (the "paired-by-being-next" axis).
- **What this confirms.** The single sn nucleus produces *all three meanings* in Egyptian under one word, and Arabic's separate surface roots for each meaning all share the sn or s+n core. The polysemy is not coincidence; it is the framework's *prediction*: the nucleus *should* produce a matched-pair-semantic cluster, and the cluster spans tooth/sister/second exactly because each of these is a matched-pair instance.

### Worked example 3.2 · Egyptian *mwt* = mother + die

- **Egyptian word.** *mwt* (the determinative distinguishes the two senses in writing).
- **Two Arabic-distinct meanings:** أُمّ (mother), مات (die).
- **Shared binary nucleus:** مو / m-w — *gathering + binding* — the *gathered-sealed-completion* seed.
- **How the two meanings derive from one seed:**
  - **Mother** — the originating gathered-source from which life emerges (gathering-as-origination).
  - **Die** — the final gathered-sealed-completion (gathering-as-conclusion).
- **What this confirms.** Birth and death are the two endpoints of the *gathering-and-completion* axis. The seed produces both *origination* (face of inception) and *termination* (face of closure) cleanly. Modern Arabic has separated the two senses onto morphologically-distinct roots, but the underlying seed-unity is preserved in Egyptian.

### Worked example 3.3 · Egyptian *ḥtp* = rest + offerings + content

- **Egyptian word.** *ḥtp*
- **Three Arabic-equivalent meanings:** حَطّ (settle / alight), قُربان (offerings), راضي (content / satisfied).
- **Shared binary nucleus:** حط / ḥt — *warm-living containment + heavy spreading touch* — the *settling-down-into-warm-completion* seed.
- **How the three meanings derive from one seed:**
  - **Rest/alight** — the body settles down into containment (the physical settling).
  - **Offerings** — what brings the heart to rest (the offering settles the relationship).
  - **Content** — the heart at rest in its containment (the affect of settledness).
- **What this confirms.** A single seed produces a cluster spanning physical action, ritual practice, and affect — all on the *settling-into-warm-completion* axis.

### Worked example 3.4 · Egyptian *jt* = father + uncle + ancestor

- Single Egyptian word covers the entire "originating-line-anchor" semantic field.
- Arabic separates أَب (father), عَمّ (paternal uncle), خال (maternal uncle), جَدّ (grandfather), سَلَف (ancestor).
- The Egyptian word reveals that these are all *one seed* (the originating-line-anchor); Arabic morphology has elaborated the field while preserving the underlying unity in the doubled form جَدّ-أَب "grandfather" and ابن-جَدّ "ancestor."

### What the methodological literature missed

Polysemy is typically treated by lexicographers as a problem (which sense is "primary"?) rather than as data (the polysemy itself reveals the underlying seed). The framework inverts this: polysemy in one descendant is *evidence* that the descendant has preserved the unified seed where other descendants have specialised morphologically. The Egyptian language, with its tendency toward bare-binary forms and minimal morphological elaboration, turns out to be unusually rich in *preserved-polysemy evidence*. This is a falsifier-passed prediction of the framework: a grammar that posits binary-nucleus seed-meanings *should* find ancient descendants that preserve the seed in unmorphologised form, and Egyptian delivers exactly that.

## 5. Implications for comparative-linguistic methodology

If the three classes of structural revelation documented above are real — and the 200-entry Afro-Asiatic roster contains 25 worked examples to weigh against the claim — then comparative methodology should be revised on three counts.

### 5.1 · Add a metathesis-check step before rejecting a cognate

When the surface skeletons of two candidate cognates don't align under standard sound-laws, the next step (before rejection) should be: *do the consonants of one form, in any permutation, share an underlying binary nucleus with the other form?* If yes, look for a third descendant that preserves the underlying order. The Coptic restoration in the لِسان/ns case is the template: if a metathesis hypothesis is correct, the language with the *less* metathesised form will usually be a descendant whose orthography is closer to the spoken form.

### 5.2 · Treat L3-variation as evidence of dual-face, not separate roots

When two surface forms share the L1+L2 binary nucleus and differ only at L3 (with both L3 candidates belonging to the same articulator class), the working assumption should be *dual-face cognate*, not separate root. The d/ḍ, t/ṭ, s/ṣ, ḥ/h alternations of comparative Semitic literature are mostly real cases of this pattern. The "two roots" treatment is a residue of pre-framework methodology.

### 5.3 · Read polysemy in one descendant as evidence about the seed, not as a disambiguation problem

When a single word in language A carries multiple meanings that correspond to distinct words in language B, the polysemy is *positive data*. It tells you that language A has preserved the underlying seed where language B has specialised. The job is to find the seed that makes the cluster sensible — and if the framework's binary nucleus produces a sensible cluster, the polysemy is itself the cognate evidence.

## 6. Limits and open questions

- **The framework was developed without Egyptian in mind.** That is its strength as a falsifier-passed test (it reads Egyptian cleanly without re-tuning). But it also means the framework's letter-charges are *Arabic-tuned*. A future test should ask: do the Egyptian *consonant inventory* and *gesture-semantics* require any extension to the 28-letter Arabic table? The early indication from the 200 entries is "no" — every entry was readable using the Arabic letter-charges as-is — but a Coptic/Egyptian-specific charge table is worth attempting.
- **Inter-rater agreement.** The 200 entries were classified by a single rater. The 84 PROVISIONAL verdicts in particular are where a second-rater pass would most likely shift the distribution. A Pass-2 calibrated re-scoring (the methodology we already validated on the Indo-European roster) is the natural next quality-control step.
- **The "structural revelations" are only as strong as the framework that produces them.** If the binary-nucleus thesis fails on independent grounds, the metathesis / dual-face / polysemy resolutions also collapse. The 25 worked examples here are *consequences* of the framework, not independent justifications of it. The independent justification lives in the operative-grammar work on 2,285 trilateral Arabic roots.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Surface-skeleton matching is a useful first filter for cognate identification, but it is not sufficient. Three classes of genuine cognate — metathesis, dual-face L3 swap, and polysemy as shared-seed evidence — are systematically missed by methodology that compares form-to-form rather than nucleus-to-meaning. The operative-grammar layer is not optional. It is the only level at which these three classes become visible.

For Afro-Asiatic comparative work specifically, the practical implication is that the existing comparative literature has been *under-counting* cognates. The 200-entry Arabic ↔ Ancient-Egyptian roster, with its 25 structural-revelation cases, suggests that the genuine cognate-count is meaningfully higher than what surface methodology has reported. A full re-pass of the existing Semitic-Egyptian comparative corpora through the operative-grammar lens is the natural next research project.
