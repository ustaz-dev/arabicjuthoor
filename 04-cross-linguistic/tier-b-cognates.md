# Tier-B Cross-Linguistic Cognate Roster · 50 Curated Findings

## What Tier-B is — and what it is not

**Tier-A** is the gold-standard roster: 19 cognates that clear all four bars of the [verification rubric](tafsir-coran-tier-a-cognates.md#the-four-bar-verification-rubric--ضوابط-التحقّق-الأربعة) (skeleton match, nucleus identity, modern reader-recognition, dual-family confirmation), with a Quranic anchor for each. Tier-A is the row a working translator can stand on.

**Tier-B** is what the bulk Eye-2 pipeline surfaced and a human curator confirmed as semantically substantial. From the 1,578 cognate findings at score ≥ 0.5 produced by the [seven-branch Eye-2 audit](../05-audits/2026-05-20-cross-branch-eye2-audit.md), this document hand-picks **50 entries from the 134 high-score pool (≥ 0.8)** that the curator (the project author) judges to be:

- clear semantic matches, not chance phonetic resemblance,
- substantial enough that an informed reader can see the shared concept once it is named,
- diverse across the seven IE branches the framework has covered.

Tier-B does **not** carry the Quranic-anchor requirement of Tier-A, and does **not** undergo the four-bar verification process. A Tier-B entry can be:
- a Mediterranean Wanderwort (an ancient trade word that spread across the contact zone), or
- a deep cognate (a shared root pre-dating documented contact), or
- a borrowing whose direction is itself unsettled.

Treat Tier-B as a curated reading list, not a verified roster.

## Selection method (transparency)

1. **Source pool:** all 134 Eye-2 findings at semantic_score ≥ 0.8 across the seven branches.
2. **Exclude:** pure proper names (place names, person names) that are simply transliterations, modern technical loans, and multiple variants of the same root that would inflate counts.
3. **Aim for branch diversity:** despite Greek having 90/134 entries in the source pool, this roster caps Greek at ~25 to ensure the other six branches are visible.
4. **Aim for semantic-field diversity:** tools, plants, animals, abstractions, body, time, weight, conflict, religious vocabulary, weights/measures.
5. **One curator pass** — no inter-rater confirmation, in the same honest spirit as Tier-A's pre-Tier-A pipeline.

Score ranges in the table: 0.95-0.98 = near-identical (verbatim equivalent under Khshim's substitutions); 0.85-0.94 = strong semantic match; 0.80-0.84 = clear match with some semantic specialisation.

## What this roster lets a translator do

Where Tier-A says "Latin *clamare* and English *claim* match Arabic كَلام at the L-K skeleton — use *claim* or *clamour* when translating," Tier-B opens a richer working palette without the four-bar warranty:

- A translator working on a passage about a *plough* can choose between *aratrum* (Latin), *ἕλκηθρον* (Greek), *plógr* (Norse), all under the Arabic ح-ر-ث / ف-ل-ج family.
- A passage about *sandalwood* or *sandals* can be rendered with deliberate skeleton-resonance to σάνδαλον.
- The Sabbath verses (Q 2:65, 4:154, 16:124) can be translated with the deliberate three-branch sounding-board of σάββατον / sabbate / sabat.

The full per-entry Eye-2 reasoning is available via the [discoveries dashboard](../discoveries.html) — filter by language and search by the Arabic root to surface the curator's notes that fed each pick.

## What Tier-B does not yet have

- **Inter-rater confirmation.** A second curator would likely upgrade some entries and downgrade others. The score gives a numeric anchor; the curator's judgement-call rests on it but does not duplicate it.
- **Quranic anchoring.** Tier-A entries each carry a verse reference. Some Tier-B entries do (سبت, الفرات, الفرخ); most do not. The Quranic-anchor pass is the natural next promotion-to-Tier-A workflow.
- **Sound-law calibration.** Khshim's nine laws were tested informally; with 1,578 findings on hand they can now be tested statistically — which law fires most often, which has the strongest semantic-match correlation. That audit is a separate item on the next-steps list.
