# The Original Tongue

**Author:** Yassine Temessek · Temessek for Research, Publishing & Training
**Status:** Position paper · Draft v1 · open to peer review
**Framework:** Juthoor (The Arabic Tongue) Operative Grammar
**Bilingual:** [النسخة العربية](the-original-tongue-ar.md)

## The claim, stated plainly

The bond between a word's sound and its meaning is not arbitrary. Each consonant carries a charge drawn from the physical gesture that makes it — where the tongue sits, where the breath stops, where the sound is shaped in the mouth — and the meaning of a root is built, gesture by gesture, from those charges. This is demonstrable *inside* Arabic, across the whole lexicon, without forcing.

When the same reading is carried out on other languages — using the **same fixed charges**, not new ones invented to fit — the same gestures keep building the same meanings, far past the point where borrowing or chance could account for it. The reading the evidence points to is this: these languages are not unrelated systems that happened to land on the same sounds. They are **branches that inherited a single original tongue** whose sound-meaning logic Qur'anic Arabic preserved most fully. We call that source **the original Arabic** — not the Arabic of any century, but the tongue whose logic Arabic kept when the other branches let it erode.

This paper sets out the evidence for that reading, in the order the framework builds it.

---

## The idea is not new

The intuition is twelve centuries old. **Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi** built *Kitab al-Ayn* — the first Arabic dictionary — ordered by place of articulation, from the deepest point of the throat forward to the lips, on the principle that the physical gesture of pronunciation is itself the wellspring of meaning. **Ibn Jinni** gave it a name in *al-Khasais*, under the chapter he called «إمساس الألفاظ أشباه المعاني» — *letters touch upon the resemblances of meanings*. **Ibn Faris**, in *Maqayis al-Lugha*, assigned every root a single core semantic value. The modern lexical ground is **Dr. Muhammad Hassan Jabal's** *al-Mu'jam al-Ishtiqaqi al-Mu'assal*, alongside **Hassan Abbas's** work on the sensory profiles of the letters.

What this project adds is not the intuition but the **systematic test**: the unified charge table, the 453 nuclei, the closed set of composition modes, and the cross-branch reading carried out at scale, root by root. The old masters saw the law; the work here is to measure it — and, in measuring it, to follow it outward to where it points.

## I. The law, proven inside Arabic

The foundation is not a hypothesis under test here; it is the established result the rest of the argument stands on.

- **28 letters, one ruling charge each**, read from the articulation gesture, with the general dual-face rule: every letter carries two semantic faces of one physical articulation, and the third radical of a trilateral root selects which face is active. Established by induction across all **453 binary nuclei**.
- **Eleven native composition modes** (plus a twelfth label, LOANWORD, for the single non-native root in the set) by which the binary nucleus operates on the third letter. **All 2,285 graded trilateral roots fit, without forcing.**

That is the law: meaning-structure bound to articulation-structure, shown at scale on a language whose root morphology stays transparent. Everything below is what happens when that same law is turned outward.

> **Honest status of the core.** All 2,285 roots now carry a complete letter-by-letter reading; the final 302 (weak-letter, hollow, hamzated and defective roots) were derived from the fixed charges and anchored to Jabal's recorded axial meanings. An independent blind re-rating (2026-07-06) then tested the one layer that is a judgment call: which of the eleven operative modes each root takes. The charges, the nucleus readings, and the Jabal anchors were given to the second reader, not tested. The result, reported as measured: two readers agree on the exact mode 37.9% of the time (Cohen's kappa 0.305, "fair") and on the broad stance 61.8%. So the fine-grained mode is single-rater-interpretive, not yet reproducible; the disagreement concentrates on an over-broad OPERATE mode (24.4% of Pass 1, kept by only 28.5% of the blind re-read). The eleven-mode taxonomy needs an example-anchored coding manual and a tighter OPERATE before it reproduces, the refinement the operative-grammar file already flagged as open. Full numbers in [the audit](../05-audits/2026-07-06-pass2-inter-rater-rerating.md).

## II. The preservation gradient — not a decay curve

Turn the fixed charges on the related languages and a gradient appears. It is tempting, from the standpoint of mainstream comparative method, to read that gradient as a signal fading into noise with distance — and to conclude that strong matches at great distance must be illusions. **That is the arbitrariness thesis reasserting itself, and it is exactly what this project tests rather than assumes.**

Read correctly, the gradient measures something else: **how much of the original tongue's sound-meaning logic each branch kept.**

| Branch | What it is | What the charges find |
|---|---|---|
| **Hebrew / Aramaic** | immediate Semitic sisters | ~84% / ~97% top-tier — the **unity baseline**: the closest surviving dialects of the one original tongue, the near end of the preservation gradient. High because they kept the most, and the floor everything else is measured against. |
| **Ancient Egyptian** | Afro-Asiatic super-family, ~6,000-year split | ~55% top-tier across 324 worked pairs — a real cross-family reach where shared vocabulary is not obvious. |
| **Coptic** | the spoken descendant of Egyptian | the **third witness**: it restores consonants the consonantal hieroglyphic script dropped (لِسان *l-s-n* ↔ hieroglyphic *ns* ↔ Coptic ⲗⲁⲥ *las* — the ل reappears, the cognate closes). |
| **Sanskrit** | the most distant branch tested | 30 worked entries, **11 PERFECT · 18 STRONG · 1 PARTIAL** read through the charges — several exact (قرن↔*karṇa*, منى↔*man*, طنّ↔*tan*, نخس↔*nakha*). |

The point is not that the numbers fall. It is that **even on the most distant branch, the core vocabulary still composes** — kin, body, number, the basic acts. A signal that simply decayed with distance would not do that. A logic that was *inherited and then variably preserved* would do exactly this: kept whole where a branch stayed conservative, scattered where it drifted, but never gone from the deepest layer.

## III. The structure travels, not just the words

Three findings show it is the *system* that the branches carry, not a handful of look-alike words:

- **Metathesis as a prediction, not an excuse.** Because the binary nucleus is unordered until the third radical attaches and selects a face, the framework *predicts* when two branches should show the same charges in a different order and still read to the same meaning. The probability of random metathesis-and-reconvergence is vanishingly small; shared structure is the parsimonious reading.
- **Dual-face splits that align across branches.** The same letter showing its two faces in cognate roots across languages is the nucleus behaving as the framework says it must — not an accident of each language separately.
- **Polysemy as a shared seed.** Where one nucleus produces a cluster of related meanings in one branch (e.g. the *s-n* matched-pair family: tooth / second / sister), and the other branch's separate surface roots each carry one member of that cluster, the polysemy is the framework's prediction, not coincidence.

When the *rules* of the system reappear in the branches — and not merely its outputs — common inheritance is the simpler explanation than parallel invention.

## IV. The universal floor

Go as far as a branch with a completely different writing system and structure — Chinese — and root-level matches thin out. But the deepest stratum still holds: the words a human infant forms first, before any culture shapes them — *mother, I, this* — reach for the **same mouth-gesture across the branches that drifted furthest apart**. Murdock's survey of 1,072 languages found the word for *mother* uses a nasal (m / n) in the overwhelming majority, far above chance; Jakobson explained why — the **m** is the sound a nursing infant makes with the lips closed. The framework reads م as *gathering, the gathered source* — the same conclusion, from first principles.

This is the floor of the gradient: the original tongue's logic at its most indestructible. It is not zero. It is universal.

---

## V. What this points to

Put together — the law proven inside Arabic, the preservation gradient, the structure that travels with the words, and the universal floor — the reading the evidence supports is that the branches descend from **one original tongue**, and that Qur'anic Arabic kept its sound-meaning logic more intact than any branch yet tested. That is the boldest reading; it is also the most parsimonious account of why the same gestures keep building the same meanings across languages the standard tree holds apart.

**On epistemic status, said honestly.** This is the framework's reading, not a claim already accepted by mainstream historical linguistics — which begins from arbitrariness and from a tree that places these families apart. The project does not defer to that starting point; it tests it. The honesty bar is held a different way: every cross-branch entry traces to a real attested form (Monier-Williams, Crum/KELLIA, the Semitic lexica), graded by whether the gesture genuinely composes — pruned where it does not, never padded. The common-origin reading is offered for examination on that evidence, not asserted past it.

## VI. The program from here

The capstone is a reading, and a reading earns its weight by what it survives. The work that deepens it:

1. **Re-rate the core, with a sharper manual.** The first independent re-rating is done (2026-07-06): mode agreement is only fair (Cohen's kappa 0.305), driven by an over-broad OPERATE mode. The next strengthening is an example-anchored coding manual and a tightened OPERATE, then a fresh blind re-rate to raise reproducibility, on the mode layer only; the charges and nucleus readings are not what the re-rating tested.
2. **Widen the branches** — carry the fixed-charge reading into Greek, Latin, Old Persian, Akkadian, Berber, each entry anchored to an attested form and graded by composition.
3. **Deepen the witnesses** — build the Coptic and Egyptian materials into full worked-entry sets, as the Sanskrit study already is.
4. **Open it to challenge** — present the rosters and the readings for peer review, declined candidates included, so the gradient can be tested rather than admired.

The claim is large on purpose. It is meant to be read against the data, root by root — which is the only place it can be confirmed or broken.
