## Interpretation

- **Unweighted κ = 0.058** is below the substantial-agreement threshold. The raters disagree on bucket assignments more than the 4-category structure can absorb. A re-prompt for the 0.50/0.65 boundary or a coarser bucketing (e.g., binary: ≥0.65 vs <0.65) is the natural next step.
- **Quadratic-weighted κ = 0.075** treats one-bucket disagreements (e.g., 'likely' vs 'plausible') as small errors rather than full disagreements. Where this exceeds unweighted κ, much of the apparent disagreement is between adjacent buckets rather than across distant ones.
- **Pearson r = 0.217** on raw 0–1 scores measures linear agreement before bucketing. Higher r means the raters' continuous-score gradients run in the same direction even where bucket boundaries split them.
- **Mean absolute difference = 0.357**: on the 0–1 scale, the average per-pair score gap. Smaller is better.

## What this audit settles

This is a **serious negative finding** for the project's per-pair score reliability — reported honestly.

1. **The framework's per-pair scores depend strongly on the rater.** Cohen's κ = 0.058 is "slight" agreement; the 4-bucket bucket assignments disagree more than the bucket structure can absorb. Gemini, blind to Sonnet's scores and operating under the same rubric, produces a substantially different distribution: where Sonnet rated 123 of 150 sample pairs as "likely" (0.65–0.79), Gemini rated only 12 in that bucket — re-routing 61 of them down to "weak" and 32 up to "strong".

2. **The asymmetry is not pure error in either direction.** Looking at the most-disagreed cases:
   - **Gemini catches some Sonnet false positives** (e.g., الشمس ↔ παραλληλισμός, idx 103, A=0.87 / B=0.00 — Sonnet was over-lenient on a spurious phonetic match).
   - **Sonnet catches some Gemini dismissals** (e.g., دحر ↔ Old Norse *aldr*, idx 114, A=0.95 / B=0.00 — Gemini dismisses a Tier-B cognate that is philologically defensible at the age/era/lifetime concept).

3. **The aggregate distribution claims should be tempered.** The 770-cognate-at-≥0.65 figure (and its bucket breakdowns: 134 ≥0.8, 57 ≥0.95) reflects what the Sonnet+Codex+Opus pipeline produced on this corpus. Different rater families would produce different aggregate counts — likely smaller from Gemini-only, perhaps comparable from Codex (which is closer to Sonnet in family).

4. **The findings the project headlines** (the Tier-A 19, the Tier-B 50, the per-language z-scores, the Khshim laws audit) **remain meaningful but with sharpened caveat:** they are findings *as scored by the original pipeline rubric*. A philologist working from the data should expect roughly half of borderline (0.65–0.80) findings to look weaker on a Gemini-style independent re-read. Strong findings (≥0.95) hold up better — of the 27 'strong' pairs in this sample, 15 (56%) survive into Gemini's 'strong' bucket too.

5. **Concrete next steps the methodology now needs:**
   - Surface a **multi-rater-confirmed subset**: pairs where ≥ 2 raters (Sonnet, Gemini, Opus) all agree the score is ≥ 0.65. This is the "high-IRR roster" that can be quoted with certainty.
   - Add **rater attribution** to all per-pair scores in the discoveries dashboard.
   - When promoting from Tier-B → Tier-A, require multi-rater agreement on the verse-anchored entry as a hard gate (in addition to the four-bar rubric).
   - Re-run a **calibration pass** on the scoring rubric — the 0.65 threshold may be too permissive for cross-family agreement.

## Caveats

- The sample is stratified random from the ≥0.65 pool, so it is well-populated at the 'likely+' end and lighter at the 'weak' end. Agreement could be lower if a wider score range were sampled.
- Rater B (Gemini) scored in batches of 30 to manage context length. Batch boundaries do not affect per-pair scores but may introduce small calibration drift across batches.
- The bucket thresholds (0.50 / 0.65 / 0.80) match the published rubric. Different thresholds would yield different κ.
