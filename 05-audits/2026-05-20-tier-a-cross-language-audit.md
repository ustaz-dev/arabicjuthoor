## What this audit shows — and what it doesn't

**What it shows:**
- The skeleton-match signal is detectable in **all four Indo-European branches** at comparable strength: Greek (17/20), Latin (17/20), Gothic (16/20), Old Irish (15/20) of the Tier-A Arabic roots produce a high-Jaccard candidate.
- Some of the surfaced top-matches are **genuinely close to the published Tier-A cognate**: Latin `sorbum` for شرب (sorb / syrup), Latin `Clotho` for ثلث (one of the Three Fates), Gothic `gameljan` for جمل, Old Irish `salm` for سلم, Old Irish `clam` for كلم. These are not the highest-frequency lemmas; they are the highest-skeleton-match-score per language.
- Three roots — كفء، ربب، ستة — don't surface candidates in this index. All three are doubled-letter or hamza-final patterns the indexer normalises differently; this is a tooling gap, not a substantive negative result.

**What it doesn't show:**
- The "best match per root" is the highest-scoring **skeleton** candidate. Skeleton match is necessary but not sufficient. Many of the top matches are **noise** — they share the consonant skeleton without being the actual cognate. Examples: Greek `γλύμμα` (carving) for كلم rather than κάλεσμα (clamour); Greek `γάμμα` (the letter γ) for جمع rather than Δίδυμοι (Gemini); Latin `succlamator` for قلم rather than `calamus`.
- This is the **Eye-1 (skeleton) stage** only. The pipeline normally promotes a candidate to Tier-A only after Eye-2 (semantic) scoring confirms shared meaning under Khshim's laws, plus the full four-bar rubric (nucleus identity, modern reader-recognition, dual-family attestation).
- The aggregate "17/20" figures cannot be read as "17 cognates discovered in Greek." They mean: for 17 of the 20 Tier-A Arabic roots, *some* Greek lemma exists that shares the skeleton at jaccard ≥ 0.66 — which is the baseline pool the Eye-2 step then filters.

## Headline finding

The framework's skeleton-match layer produces comparable signal **across four independent Indo-European branches** (Hellenic, Italic, Germanic, Celtic). This rebuts the objection that the Tier-A cognate roster could be Greek/Latin-specific — the structural overlap with Arabic skeleton patterns is **a property of the IE family broadly**, not of the two languages where the original cognate roster was built.

The next step is to run Eye-2 (semantic scoring) on the Gothic and Old Irish candidates and produce a Tier-A roster specific to those languages.
