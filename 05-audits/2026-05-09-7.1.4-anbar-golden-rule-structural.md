# The reversibility test — structural pass

**Date:** 2026-05-09
**Hypothesis tested:** if R = (a, b, c) is a trilateral root, the reverse R' = (c, b, a) carries an inverted or related meaning to R.

**This pass:** structural existence only. For every trilateral root in Jabal's lexicon, we test whether its 5 non-identity permutations exist as roots. Semantic relatedness is the next pass (graded separately).

## Next pass — semantic grading

The 155 reverse pairs are the canonical test of Anbar's Golden Rule. For each pair, an LLM (or human) should grade the semantic relationship between Axial A and Axial B on a 4-level scale:

  - **inverse**  — meanings are clearly opposed (the strong-Anbar case)
  - **related**  — meanings share a recognizable conceptual axis but aren't strictly inverse (the weak-Anbar case)
  - **adjacent** — meanings are in the same domain but the link is loose
  - **unrelated** — meanings have no recognizable connection

Anbar's rule is **confirmed** if `inverse + related` materially exceeds the rate expected by chance (controlling for shared-letter coincidences). This grading is the LV2/LV3 follow-up to this structural pass.
