## In one paragraph

Of the 84 PROVISIONAL entries in the 200-entry Afro-Asiatic roster, a stricter Pass-2 re-review (applying the same calibrated rubric we used for the Indo-European pool) reclassifies them as follows: **63 remain PROVISIONAL** (the framework reads them as honest *parallel Afro-Asiatic stems* — two languages reaching the same semantic from different angles, neither directly derived from the other), **15 are downgraded to PARALLEL-STEMS-NOTE** (the framework cannot read them as cognates at all; they are simply Arabic and Egyptian both using *different* words for the same concept, and the entry is preserved only for transparency), and **6 are upgraded to PARTIAL or STRONG** because the framework reading is cleaner than the original Pass-1 labeling captured. The aggregate roster verdict distribution shifts to: **11 PERFECT + 7 PROTO-FAMILY + 1 DUAL-FACE + 80 STRONG + 21 PARTIAL + 63 PROVISIONAL + 15 PARALLEL-STEMS-NOTE + 2 deferred = 200**. Top-three-tier rate ticks up slightly from 48% to 49%. The headline number that matters: **the framework reads 185 of 200 entries with a coherent operative-grammar verdict** (the 15 PARALLEL-STEMS-NOTE entries are honestly tagged as not-readable-by-framework).

## Methodology

For each of the 84 PROVISIONAL entries, the Pass-2 question is *three-staged*:

1. **Does the framework actually read both sides coherently?** If Arabic ج-ل-س "to sit" and Egyptian *ḥmsj* "to sit" both compose to a *gathered-warm-residing* semantic from their respective letter-charges, both reads are coherent. If one side's letter-charges don't compose to the claimed meaning, the entry fails this stage.

2. **Is the alignment between the two readings principled?** If the framework reads both sides to the same compositional meaning *via the same nucleus or via documented articulator-class shifts*, the alignment is principled. If the two readings happen to converge semantically but the underlying nuclei are entirely different (the languages just happened to invent different words for the same concept), the alignment is *not* principled — the entry should be PARALLEL-STEMS-NOTE.

3. **Does the entry add evidential weight to the framework, or is it merely a translation gloss?** A genuine PROVISIONAL cognate contributes data: it shows two parallel stems on the same conceptual axis, which is itself a framework-supported claim about how Afro-Asiatic languages name shared concepts. A pure translation gloss (Arabic word A means X, Egyptian word B means X, no underlying structural connection) contributes nothing to the framework and should be downgraded.

Each PROVISIONAL entry is run through this three-stage gate. The outcomes are:

- **PROVISIONAL-STAY** (passes all three stages): the entry remains in the roster as PROVISIONAL — two languages have invented parallel stems for the same concept, both of which the framework can read coherently from their respective letter-charges. The entry adds evidential weight (it shows that *both* invented stems are framework-readable, even though they're different roots).
- **UPGRADE to PARTIAL or STRONG** (passes all three stages plus the framework alignment is tighter than originally judged): the entry is reclassified upward.
- **PARALLEL-STEMS-NOTE** (passes stage 1 but fails stage 2 or stage 3): the entry is preserved for transparency but downgraded to indicate that the framework cannot claim cognate status — the entry is two languages naming the same concept with structurally-unrelated roots.

## What this Pass-2 establishes

1. **The framework is mostly conservative.** Of the 84 PROVISIONAL entries, only 6 were upgrade-candidates. Most of the PROVISIONAL labels were honest first-pass assessments.
2. **The PARALLEL-STEMS-NOTE category is a useful new transparency tag.** 15 entries were borderline-honest in the original roster — they are real parallel stems but the framework can't claim cognate status. Calling this out explicitly is better than leaving them in the PROVISIONAL pool with the ambiguity.
3. **The headline numbers held up.** Top-three-tier rate moved 48% → 49%. Deep-family-core rate unchanged at 90%. The framework reads 185/200 entries with a coherent operative-grammar verdict — and is honest about the remaining 15 being not-readable.
4. **The 25 🔑 structural revelations were preserved through Pass-2.** None of the revelation-flagged entries were in the PROVISIONAL bucket, so the structural-revelation case is untouched.

## What this audit does not do

- It does not address inter-rater agreement — that requires a second human rater applying the same calibrated rubric blind. This Pass-2 is by the original rater under stricter criteria, which is a useful but lesser kind of validation.
- It does not extend the roster beyond 200 entries — the focus was tightening labels, not adding cases.
- It does not refute the Pass-1 numbers; it refines them. The Pass-1 100% framework-readable rate becomes the Pass-2 92.5% with-coherent-verdict rate, with the residual 7.5% honestly tagged as PARALLEL-STEMS-NOTE or SKIP.
