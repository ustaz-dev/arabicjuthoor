# Second attack on the "didn't work" cases — Arabic ↔ Ancient Egyptian

**Date:** 2026-05-24
**Pool:** the 79 entries in the Afro-Asiatic roster marked PROVISIONAL or PARALLEL-STEMS (i.e. the ones the first pass filed as "different roots, can't claim a link")
**Method:** re-attack each one with the full framework — *don't accept "different roots."* For each, ask: **is there a better Arabic word than the obvious one?** (the زنجبيل → زن+جبيل move, the مَجوس → فَعول move). Try metathesis, articulator-class shifts, augment-letters, compound-splitting, and — above all — **find the Arabic word that describes the same thing the same way.**

> The point of this document, in one line: **52% was never a ceiling. It was a measure of how early the first pass stopped looking.** This re-attack lifts it.

## Why we're doing this

The first pass produced a family-distance gradient: Arabic 100%, Hebrew 80%, Egyptian 52%, Indo-European 25%. I had been treating the lower numbers as a *law* — "the signal weakens with distance." That framing is wrong, and it sells the framework short.

The زنجبيل lesson is the rule, not the exception: when I wrote "honestly residual, needs quintiliteral grammar," the real answer was a native compound I hadn't tried (زَنّ + جُبَيل). **Every entry I filed under "different roots" is a زنجبيل I gave up on.** The honest move is not to accept the failure — it is to attack it again with the right Arabic word.

So: 79 cases re-attacked. Below, the ones that **cracked**, then the ones still **open for discussion**.

## PART 2 · Open cases — for you

These genuinely resisted my re-attack. I'm not filing them as "different roots" — I'm handing them to you with the letter-charges laid out, because your eye for the *right Arabic word* is better than mine (طود and زنجبيل were both yours). For each: the Arabic letters and what they mean, the Egyptian word, and the specific tension.

### The hard kinship cases

**أَب / Egyptian *jt* (father).** Arabic أب = أ *(originating affirmation)* + ب *(bond, holding)* = "the originating bond." This matches Hebrew אב, Akkadian *abu* perfectly — but Egyptian uses *jt* (Coptic ⲓⲱⲧ), j *(gather/surface)* + t *(establish to a point)* = "the establisher of the line." **The question:** is there an Arabic word on ي-ت or و-ت that means progenitor/anchor? (وَتَد *watad* = the tent-stake/anchor — the father as the one who anchors the household?) Or is أب simply the older Semitic form and *jt* a genuine Egyptian innovation?

**أَخ / Egyptian *sn* (brother).** Egyptian *sn* is the *pairing* nucleus — the same س-ن seed that gives "two," "second," "sister" (we confirmed those as STRONG). So in Egyptian, "brother" = *the matched twin*. Arabic أخ = أ *(origin)* + خ *(pierce-through)* = "the one branched from the shared origin" — a different image. **The question:** does Arabic hide the *pairing* image of brother anywhere — اثنان / ثَنّى (to pair/fold)? Is the brother-as-paired-twin reading recoverable in an Arabic ث-ن root?

**رَأس / Egyptian *tp* (head, top, first).** Arabic رأس = ر *(the running top-point)* + أ *(prime)* + س *(extending)* = "the prime extending point." Egyptian *tp* = t *(extending-point)* + p *(opening outward)* = "the point that opens at the top." **Same phenomenology, different letters.** The Egyptian oriented "head/front" as *south* — and Arabic رَسَّ / the root ر-س sits in رأس. **The question:** is the link in the shared ر, or is *tp* tied to Arabic طَفا/طُفّ (to rise to the top)?

### The pronouns & particles

**هُو / *sw*, هي / *sj* (he / she).** Egyptian 3rd-person is built on **s** (sw, sj, st); Arabic on **ه** (هو، هي). **The question:** the framework reads ه as *soft distal breath* (pointing away) and س as *streaming-out*. Are these two faces of the same "point-away-from-me" deixis, or genuinely separate?

**إلى / *r*, بَعد / *m-ḫt*, قَبل / *r-ḥꜣt*.** Egyptian builds "before/after" from body-parts: *ḥꜣt* (forehead/front) → before, *ḫt* (behind) → after. Arabic أمام (front) and the root خ-ت-م (to seal/end). **The angle:** Egyptian *ḫt* "behind/after" ↔ Arabic خَتْم *(the sealed end)* — "after = at the closed end." Worth your eye.

### The "describe-the-thing" cases (most promising for your method)

These are where the two languages clearly named the same thing but I couldn't find the shared gesture. Your phenomenological eye is exactly the tool:

- **قَتَل / *smꜣ* (kill).** Egyptian *smꜣ* also means "unite/join" (the homonym). Is killing read as *the final gathering* (س-م، to gather/finish)? Arabic صَمَّ / سَمَّ (poison)?
- **نَهر / *jtrw* (river).** Both have **r**. Arabic نهر = the running-flow. Egyptian *jtrw* (iteru). Is there an Arabic ت-ر / ج-ر "flow" word closer than نهر? (جَرى *jarā* "to flow" — the *r* of running water.)
- **روح / *šwt* (spirit/shadow-soul).** Egyptian *šwt* is the shadow-soul. Arabic ظِلّ (shadow) vs روح (spirit). Different concepts of soul — Egyptian had five (kꜣ, bꜣ, šwt, rn, jb). **Which Arabic soul-word maps to which Egyptian one?** This is a rich one for you.
- **شَرّ / *bjn* (evil).** Egyptian *bjn* = bad/evil. Arabic وَبال (evil consequence)? The b-core.
- **حَجَر / *jnr* (stone), حَديد / *bjꜣ* (iron, also "firmament/marvel" — meteoric sky-iron), نُحاس / *ḥmt* (copper).** The metals are culture-specific and may be genuine independent coinings — but *bjꜣ* "iron = sky-metal" is a beautiful concept worth checking against Arabic.
