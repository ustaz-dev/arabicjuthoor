## What this tests

The framework's claim is that Eye-1 (consonant-skeleton match) and Eye-2 (semantic-meaning match) correlate. If real Arabic ↔ IE cognates exist, pairs with high skeleton-match scores should also have high semantic-match scores. If skeleton-match is just phonetic chance, the two should be uncorrelated.

For each language we compute the Spearman rank correlation between Eye-1's discovery_score and Eye-2's semantic_score across all scored pairs in that language. Then we permute the semantic scores 1000 times and recompute the correlation each time to get a null distribution. The z-score is (observed − null_mean) / null_std. Under approximate normality of the permutation distribution, z ≥ 3.29 corresponds to p < 0.001.

### What the result says

- A high positive z-score per language means: in that language's corpus, Eye-1 skeleton matches and Eye-2 semantic confirmations are NOT independent. The framework picks out the skeleton matches that semantic review also confirms.
- A low or negative z-score means: in that language, skeleton-match and semantic-match are uncorrelated — the framework's signal at Eye-1 doesn't survive Eye-2 review, and the apparent cognates may be phonetic artefacts.
- The branch-specific test is sharper than the combined z=3.23 because it isolates which IE branches show genuine cross-linguistic correspondence with Arabic, and which do not.

## Caveats

- This is a permutation test on the score-pair distribution. It tests the framework's *consistency* (high-skeleton predicts high-semantic), not whether the absolute semantic scores are calibrated correctly.
- Languages with very few Eye-2 records (n < 30) are skipped. The combined-Greek+Latin baseline is computed separately for direct comparison with the original z = 3.23.
- Old Norse and Welsh use the Wave-2 detective-verdict pipeline. We map verdicts to scores (confirmed_cognate=0.95, plausible_link=0.70, shared_loanword=0.55, proper_name=0.30, false_positive=0.05) for comparability. This is a deliberate flattening of a richer ordinal scale.
